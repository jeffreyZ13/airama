What lines up, and what doesn't

  • Across the four tracks there is one note every 250 ms, handed out in turn: Gendang, Rebab, Serunai, Gong, then back to Gendang. That matches the 250 ms hop, so the export script writes one note per analysis step and rotates it across tracks.
  • Because of that rotation, a note on the Gong track isn't driven by prefrontal delta, and a Serunai note isn't driven by temporal gamma. The shaded CTM bands show what each instrument would follow under the CTM table.
  • Pitch changes in blocks shared by all four tracks: C4 in the first 12.5 s (bars 1–7), D4 from 10 s, and E4 from 26 s (bar 14). Every note is about 0.23 s long.
  • Velocity climbs from 62 to 110 over the first 7 s, then stays at 108–110 for 88% of notes, on all four tracks alike. It doesn't follow the band power at each note (|ρ| ≤ 0.11 on every track).

Reading the chart

  • Each EEG value is stamped at the end of its 2-second window, so the feature lanes start at 2 s (bar 2).
  • Eye blinks and other large frontal waves appear in almost every window (98%). They dominate the Gong's delta band and also drive the Gendang and Rebab lanes, which rise and fall with it.
  • Frontal alpha coherence is very high here (median 0.90), but it moves with the blink-dominated delta lane (ρ = 0.68), which it didn't for Subjects 01 and 02. Treat it as inflated by eye artifacts, not as strong synchrony.
  • Each lane is scaled to its own 2nd–98th percentile, so compare shapes over time rather than heights between lanes. The tooltip gives the absolute µV².
  • The sound is a mix recorded from Ableton Live with stand-in instruments, not a live link to the EEG. This recording runs 62 s: the last 2 s is the reverb and gong ringing out after bar 30, so the playhead holds at the end while it plays.
