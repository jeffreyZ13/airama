The idea

An old way of healing through sound, rebuilt as a feedback loop

In Kelantan's Main Puteri and Mak Yong, music is not background. A small ensemble led by the rebab player watches the person at the centre of the performance and shapes the music in response: slower here, a new melody there, the gong marking each cycle. The ceremony begins with Buka Panggung, the opening of the stage.

A.Irama asks what happens when that watching is done by sensors and an algorithm. A lightweight headband reads the brain. A signal-processing chain turns bursts of brain activity into musical notes. Each instrument is assigned to a part of the brain and a frequency band, so what you hear follows what the brain is doing. A reinforcement-learning agent adjusts tempo and timbre, keeping the changes that raise coherence between the left and right frontal lobes in the alpha band.

The work is an engineering architecture for one listening session. It makes no clinical claims. Whether the loop actually changes a listener's brain is a question for a controlled human study, which is designed but not yet run.

Session arc

Twenty minutes, in four phases modelled on the Buka Panggung: calibrate, entrain, sustain, resolve.

The first three minutes record a resting baseline. The agent then explores, settles on what works, and in the last two minutes slows the drum to 80 BPM for a gentle close.

How it listens

From scalp to score in four stages

The whole loop repeats every 250 ms on a two-second window of EEG, inside a 100 ms computing budget.

1 · LISTEN

Six dry electrodes

A headband reads prefrontal, frontal and temporal sites and streams them over Bluetooth.

Fp1 Fp2 AF7 AF8 T7 T8 · 256 Hz
2 · CLEAN

Filter and flag

Filters remove drift and mains hum. Blinks are regressed out of the frontal channels, and jaw-muscle noise on the temporal channels is detected.

0.5–45 Hz band-pass · notch · blink regression · EMG gate
3 · TRANSLATE

Bursts become notes

Morlet wavelets map energy in time and frequency. Each burst is fitted as a small half-ellipsoid "bump" and becomes one note on a pentatonic scale.

Sparse Bump Sonification · A-minor pentatonic
4 · CONDUCT

An agent that learns

Every 5 seconds a Q-learning agent adjusts tempo, rebab brightness or gong level, and is rewarded when the phase-lag index of frontal alpha rises.

MDP · 140 states · 7 actions · ε-greedyWatch the agent learn →

The AI conductor

An agent that learns what settles each listener

Every 5 seconds a reinforcement-learning agent changes the tempo, the rebab's brightness or the gong, then checks whether the listener's frontal synchrony rose. Tested against 200 simulated listeners carrying real EEG noise, it found better settings than a fixed one and did not take the bait of an artifact that could fake its reward.

Cultural Timbre Mapping

Every instrument listens to a different part of the brain

Gendang

Double-headed drum, ibu and anak
Listens toTheta, 4–8 Hz

The drummers' density follows theta power. Busier theta brings busier interlocking patterns.

Rebab

Three-string spike fiddle, the ensemble's leader
Listens toFrontal alpha, AF7 + AF8

The melody rises and falls with frontal alpha, the rhythm behind calm, wakeful attention.

Serunai

Quadruple-reed oboe
Listens toTemporal gamma, T7 + T8

The reed enters when high-frequency activity at the temples rises, shadowing the rebab.

Gong

Tetawak, the colotomic anchor
Listens toPrefrontal delta, Fp1 + Fp2

The gong keeps the Mak Yong 16-beat cycle, striking on beats 4, 8, 12 and 14, with a low gong on 16.

The recordings on this page use Ableton Live's built-in sounds as stand-ins: tabla samples for the gendang, a physical-model string for the rebab, an FM voice for the serunai and a resonator for the gong. Recorded Kelantan instruments will replace them.

Three listeners

One minute of three real brains, played back

Each recording is one minute of eyes-open rest from the public PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery dataset. These people never heard the music. The maps show what the system hears in their EEG and how it plays it.

The composition

Three Minds, One Stage

The raw sonification is informative but not yet music. To share it with an audience, the three recordings became three movements of one piece, set inside the structure of a Mak Yong opening. Choose a section to hear it.

Driven by the brain data
  • How busy the gendang plays, from theta power in three levels
  • The shape of the rebab melody, from frontal alpha smoothed over four seconds
  • When the serunai joins, whenever temporal gamma is above its median
  • The order of the movements, one person after another
Composed choices
  • The mode: D within a fifth, with C and B♭ as neighbour tones
  • The 16-beat Mak Yong gong cycle and 120 BPM pulse
  • Gendang ibu and anak phrase templates
  • The opening Bertabuh and the closing Penutup

What the maps revealed

Listening closely changed the design

0.899Subject 03 coherence

The highest coherence of the three came from the recording dominated by eye movements. Blinks reach both frontal sensors at once and inflate the measure. This is why every coherence gain must be confirmed by the phase-lag index before the agent is rewarded.

2.8×Subject 02 temporal gamma

Subject 02's temple channels carried almost three times the high-frequency power of Subject 01 for the whole minute. That pattern looks like jaw-muscle tension, not brain activity, and it is exactly the case the Serunai muscle gate is built to catch.

244notes, in strict rotation

The first export script handed notes to the four instruments in turn, with a fixed length, whatever the EEG did. The maps made that visible, and the routing is now being rebuilt to follow the Cultural Timbre Mapping.

16beats in the gong cycle

Data alone did not make the output listenable. A fixed Mak Yong metric frame, with the brain steering density, contour and entries, did. The live system follows the same split: learning happens inside a stable cultural structure.
