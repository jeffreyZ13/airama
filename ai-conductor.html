A.Irama · the learning component

The AI Conductor

One 20-minute session in which the reinforcement-learning agent searches for the tempo and rebab brightness that raise a listener's frontal synchrony. You hear the agent's choices as they happen, compressed into four and a half minutes.

A simulation, not a person. The listener is a model: its synchrony peaks at a tempo and brightness the agent is not told, and its readings carry real EEG noise from 120 PhysioNet recordings. This is one of the clearer sessions (the best 18% reach three-quarters of the attainable synchrony; the median session reaches 44%). Audio is a time-lapse: each 5-second decision plays for 1.25 seconds, at the tempo the agent chose. Instruments are synthesised previews.

What the agent sees

Every 5 seconds it reads the current musical setting (tempo, brightness, gong level) and the average wPLI over the last 5 seconds, compared with the first three minutes. That comparison is its reward.

What it does

It picks one of seven moves: tempo up or down by 10 BPM, brightness up or down, gong up or down, or hold. Early on it often tries something at random (exploring, pink ticks). As its table of experience fills, it mostly repeats what paid off (exploiting).

What to listen for

  • 0:00–0:18 · the calibration phase at 110 BPM
  • 0:18–2:12 · restless tempo changes as the agent explores (session minutes 3 to 10½)
  • 2:12–4:03 · the tempo settles at 130 BPM, next to the listener's optimum of 133
  • 4:03 onward · the protocol slows the drum to 80 BPM to close

In the full study of 200 simulated sessions, this agent beat a fixed setting (p < 0.001) but not a simple hill-climbing search, and it lagged behind the fixed setting for most of the exploration phase, overtaking it only after about 11 minutes. Whether any of this helps a real listener can only be tested with people.
